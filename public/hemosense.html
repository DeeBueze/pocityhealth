HemoSense

Blood typing at the point of care. A drop of blood, three test channels and a result on screen, using impedance sensing on low-cost graphite electrodes.

Try the demo ↓ See the specs

How it works

Blood typing depends on agglutination: red cells clump when they meet the matching antibody. HemoSense detects that clumping electrically instead of by eye.

  1. 1

    Step 1: Add the sample

    A drop of blood goes onto three channels: Anti-A, Anti-B and Anti-D.

  2. 2

    Step 2: Cells react

    Where an antigen matches its antibody, the red cells agglutinate on the electrodes.

  3. 3

    Step 3: Impedance is read

    The microcontroller measures each channel and compares it with the agglutination threshold.

  4. 4

    Step 4: Group is shown

    The three outcomes combine into an ABO and Rh result on the OLED screen.

Technology

The electrodes are drawn in pencil graphite, a method supported by published research on graphite sensors for blood typing. The electronics use common, locally available parts.

Sensing method
Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy
Electrodes
Pencil graphite
Controller
ESP32-S3
Agglutination threshold
25 kOhm

Questions

Is HemoSense ready for clinical use?

No. It is a research prototype and has not been approved for clinical use.

What does it test for?

ABO and Rh blood group, using three channels: Anti-A, Anti-B and Anti-D.

Why graphite electrodes?

They are cheap, easy to produce and supported by published research, which keeps the cost of each test low.
